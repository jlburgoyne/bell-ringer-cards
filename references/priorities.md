# Priorities from the beginning-of-year test

> **Sample data.** The numbers on this page are invented to show the format. They do not describe any real class or school. Replace this page with a summary of the teacher's own class-level results.

Aggregate results from a beginning-of-year diagnostic given to the teacher's 9th, 10th, and 11th grade classes. No student information. Use this to **suggest**; the teacher decides. It stays in use until there's another test to replace it.

Scores are the percent of available points earned. Each grade took a different test, so don't compare grades point for point. Guessing on a four-choice item averages about 25%.

## 10th and 11th grade sets

### Weak in both grades: the best fit for a shared set

| Area | Standards | 10th | 11th | Regents weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Informational reading | R1, R2, R5, R8 (RI) | 48% | 41% | Part 2, the argument essay, is built on four informational sources: 24 of 56 credits. Part 1 includes a nonfiction text. |
| Conventions | L1, L2 | 45% | 40% | Control of Conventions, a scored row on both essay rubrics. |

### 10th grade

- **Conventions, lowest items:** colons and semicolons (L2), and phrases and clauses (L1), both at 30% or below.
- **Informational reading** is the clearest reading weakness.
- **Literary reading is mostly solid:** word meaning, figurative language, character, and theme in the 75 to 95% range.

### 11th grade

- **Weakest with real Regents weight:** author's choices (R3, 25%) and structure (R5, 22%). That's close to the Part 3 task: how a writing strategy develops a central idea.
- **Vocabulary:** word strategies (L4) and academic vocabulary (L6), both near 30%.
- **Relatively stronger:** figurative language (L5) and integrating sources (R7).
- When many items sit near guessing level, item-to-item differences at the bottom are unreliable; shorter or scaffolded passages may help students practice the skill before reading level gets in the way.

## 9th grade sets

9th grade doesn't take the Regents this year, so rank by how weak the skill is and how much it helps later Regents work.

- **Sections differ:** if one section is well behind another, offering multiple choice may matter more than the averages suggest. Anything weak in all sections is a priority.
- **Written responses:** full credit on only about 1 in 4 two-point written items. Restating, answering, citing evidence (R1), and explaining are the clearest need.
- **Weak in all sections:**
  - Point of view and purpose (R6).
  - Academic vocabulary (L6).
  - Central idea in informational text (R2).
  - Parallel structure (L1).
- **Relatively stronger:** word meaning from context (L4) and basic punctuation.

## How to use this

- When the teacher asks what to work on next, or makes a broad request ("something for reading"), suggest two or three options from this page, for the grade the set is for: for 10th and 11th, start with the ones weak in both grades and worth the most on the Regents; for 9th, start with the ones weak in all sections. Say briefly why. The teacher picks.
- When the teacher asks for a skill this page shows as very weak (roughly 30% or below, or near guessing), say so in one sentence and offer multiple choice. Don't switch to multiple choice unless the teacher says yes.
- Otherwise don't bring the data up. Don't suggest more testing.
